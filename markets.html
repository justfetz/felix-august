Why the ten-year matters here

Most of what this household owns is priced on earnings that arrive years from now. The ten-year yield is the market's toll on waiting: when it rises, future cash flows are worth less today and long-duration growth stocks usually feel it first; when it falls, the same holdings get a tailwind that has nothing to do with the businesses themselves.

So when the portfolio has a great or terrible month, the first question is how much of that was the companies and how much was the discount rate. This page exists so the boys can learn to separate the two.

  • Ten-year rising: expect pressure on the AI and growth sleeve, even on good company news.
  • Ten-year falling: multiples expand; do not confuse the tailwind with skill.
  • 2s10s inverted (two-year above ten-year): the bond market is bracing for a slowdown.
  • Utilities and energy often behave like bond proxies; they compete with the yield directly.

How this page is made

A scheduled GitHub Action (.github/workflows/market-data.yml) runs on weekday evenings, pulls the daily par yield curve from the U.S. Treasury and end-of-day quotes from Alpha Vantage, and commits data/market.json. The quote key lives in an Action secret; nothing with a key ever reaches the browser.
